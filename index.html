Abstract

Overview of the research problem, approach, and findings.

Burnout is an increasingly prevalent challenge in software engineering, yet its impact on software development processes remains insufficiently understood. In this paper, we introduce Emotional Debt, a novel socio-technical construct that captures the accumulation of unresolved psychological strain experienced by developers. We further operationalize this construct through Emotional Smells, a set of observable behavioral indicators that can manifest within software repositories.

To investigate the relationships among burnout, Emotional Debt, and Emotional Smells, we conducted a survey-based study of 72 software developers and analyzed the resulting data using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM). Our results show that burnout significantly contributes to the accumulation of Emotional Debt (R² = 0.613), which in turn positively influences the emergence of Emotional Smells (R² = 0.454). A significant partial mediation was also confirmed, suggesting that developer burnout damages repositories both directly and indirectly — through the progressive buildup of unaddressed emotional load.

Burnout

Operationalized via the three canonical Maslach Burnout Inventory (MBI) dimensions. Burnout is defined by WHO as an occupational phenomenon resulting from chronic unmanaged workplace stress.

Dimensions: Emotional Exhaustion (depletion of emotional resources) · Cynicism / Depersonalization (detachment from work) · Reduced Personal Efficacy (self-doubt about contributions).

Items: 9 (QID1–QID3) Cronbach's α: 0.920 AVE: 0.612 Composite Reliability: 0.934

Emotional Debt

A novel socio-technical construct — the accumulation of unresolved psychological strain by developers. Modeled through four lifecycle stages: Decision (triggered by burnout), Accumulation (latent burden), Observable Signals (Emotional Smells), and Long-term Costs (degraded maintainability).

Burnout explains 61.3% of the variance in Emotional Debt (R² = 0.613, T = 8.624, p < 0.001), with a strong path coefficient of β = 0.783.

Items: 6 (QID4_1–QID4_6) Cronbach's α: 0.875 AVE: 0.618 Composite Reliability: 0.906

Emotional Smells

Observable behavioral proxies detectable in software repositories that signal underlying Emotional Debt accumulation. Organized into three macro-dimensions: Process Smells (map to Exhaustion) · Communication Smells (map to Cynicism) · Technical Shortcut Smells (map to Inefficacy).

Emotional Debt explains 45.4% of variance in Emotional Smells (R² = 0.454). Partial mediation confirmed: both direct (β = 0.618) and indirect (β = 0.338) effects are significant.

Items: 11 (QID5_1–QID5_11) Cronbach's α: 0.899 AVE: 0.526 Composite Reliability: 0.917

Survey Constructs & Items

All 20 survey items across 4 constructs, measured on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = Never, 5 = Always).

Burnout — Exhaustion

  • QID1_1I feel mentally exhausted because of my software development work.
  • QID1_2I feel emotionally drained after working.
  • QID1_3I feel exhausted at the end of the working day.

Burnout — Cynicism

  • QID2_1I feel more detached from my work than in the past.
  • QID2_2I feel less emotionally involved in my project.
  • QID2_3I tend to distance myself from work-related interactions.

Burnout — Inefficacy

  • QID3_1I feel less effective in my development activities.
  • QID3_2Tasks that were simple before now require much more effort.
  • QID3_3I doubt the quality of my contributions.

Emotional Debt

  • QID4_1I carry unresolved work-related emotional tensions over time.
  • QID4_2Emotional stress from previous jobs continues to affect me.
  • QID4_3I work without addressing the emotional difficulties related to work.
  • QID4_4Accumulated frustration influences my work decision-making.
  • QID4_5Emotional tension persists even after tasks are completed.
  • QID4_6I feel emotionally "in debt" due to prolonged work pressure.

Emotional Smells

  • QID5_1I have repeatedly worked at night or during weekends.
  • QID5_2I made many small commits instead of few well-structured ones.
  • QID5_3I performed commits with high frequency in short intervals.
  • QID5_4My commit messages expressed frustration or negativity.
  • QID5_5I wrote commit messages with little or no explanation (e.g., "fix").
  • QID5_6I avoided commenting, reviewing, or participating in discussions.
  • QID5_7I worked on the same files or modules predominantly alone.
  • QID5_8I reworked the same code multiple times in a short period.
  • QID5_9I repeatedly reverted recent changes.
  • QID5_10I applied fixes that did not fully resolve the problem.
  • QID5_11I used AI-generated code with minimal review or adaptation.

SmartPLS 4 Analysis

Open the .splsm project in SmartPLS 4 and run: (1) PLS Algorithm, (2) Bootstrapping (5,000 subsamples, two-tailed, α = 0.05), (3) PLSpredict. All exported results are included in Pls-sem/Exports/ for independent verification.

How to Replicate

Click each step to expand. Reproduce all results from scratch.

1
Data Collection ▲

Deploy the survey instrument (see Survey/) via Qualtrics. Recruit active software developers through Prolific. Apply three quality filters: attention check, minimum completion time, active version control experience. Target at least 72 valid responses.

2
Data Preparation ▲

Export raw data from Qualtrics as survey.csv. Run: python Scripts/format_for_plssem.py --in Data/survey.csv --out Data/survey_pls.csv. Verify output row count and column set match the included survey_pls.csv.

3
Measurement Model Evaluation ▲

Open the SmartPLS project and run the PLS Algorithm. Check: outer loadings > 0.70, AVE > 0.50, Composite Reliability > 0.70, HTMT < 0.90. Compare against Pls algorithm.xlsx and Confirmatory Terad Analises.xlsx.

4
Structural Model & Bootstrapping ▲

Run Bootstrapping with 5,000 subsamples, two-tailed test, α = 0.05. Verify path coefficients (β), T-values, p-values, R², f², and VIF ratios against Bootstrapping.xlsx. Check VIF < 5 for collinearity.

5
Predictive Assessment (PLSpredict) ▲

Run PLSpredict to assess out-of-sample predictive power. For each indicator, verify that PLS RMSE < LM RMSE, confirming the structural model outperforms a naïve linear baseline. Compare against Pls predict.xlsx.

6
Mediation Analysis ▲

Use SmartPLS specific effects output to verify the indirect path: Burnout → Emotional Debt → Emotional Smells (β = 0.338, T = 2.614, p = 0.009). Confirm partial mediation: both direct and indirect effects are significant.
